"LLMOps in Practice with MLflow: Experiment Management and Quality Assurance for Generative AI Applications" will be published by Gijutsu-Hyoronsha on April 20, 2026.

https://gihyo.jp/book/2026/978-4-297-15573-5

To our knowledge, this is the first Japanese-language book dedicated to LLMOps and MLflow. The co-authors include engineers from Databricks Japan who serve on the front lines of MLflow development — as tech lead, core developers, and maintainer — bringing both the design philosophy of the tool and hands-on implementation experience directly to the page.

Building generative AI applications is accelerating, yet familiar operational challenges persist: prompt quality degradation, opaque costs, unexpected behavior in production. LLMOps is the set of practices and tools for addressing these challenges systematically, and its importance only grows as AI development matures.

We hope this book serves as a practical starting point for that work.


# Why LLMOps, Why Now

Years have passed since ChatGPT's emergence, and building something that works with an LLM is no longer a high bar. The challenge has shifted: running it reliably in production, continuously.

Many teams have run into the same walls:

- A small prompt change causes unexpected behavior
- Quality degrades and the cause is unclear
- Costs grow without visibility into where

These are challenges specific to LLM applications — ones that conventional software development practices and traditional ML workflows aren't equipped to handle. LLMOps is the body of practices built to address them.


# MLflow 3 and LLMOps

MLflow has long been a standard tool in the MLOps ecosystem, but version 3 represents a significant redesign — one that places generative AI at the center of the platform rather than treating it as an add-on.

This book is organized around MLflow 3's four core components:

- **Tracing** — Visualize the internal behavior of LLM applications and pinpoint root causes quickly
- **Evaluate & Monitor** — Quantify LLM quality and build continuous evaluation pipelines
- **Prompt Registry** — Version-control prompts and safely drive improvement cycles as a team
- **AI Gateway** — Manage multiple LLM providers uniformly, with control over cost and security

Together, these components provide the foundation for moving from "it works" to "we can operate it."


# What's Inside

The book is structured to take readers from conceptual understanding through hands-on implementation and production operations.

**Foundations (Chapters 1–3)**
The context for why LLMOps matters, MLflow's evolution from MLOps to LLMOps platform, and environment setup.

**Practitioner's Core (Chapters 4–8)**
The heart of the book. Starting from a simple chatbot, readers work through a full improvement cycle:
Tracing → Evaluation → Prompt Management → Serving → Monitoring & Operations.
Each chapter is not a standalone topic but a stage in a continuous improvement loop designed for real LLM applications.

**Operations (Chapters 9–10)**
Real-world use cases including contract information extraction, enterprise document search with agentic RAG, and multi-agent technical report generation. Also covers governance, access control, and compliance for large-scale deployments.

**Advanced Topics (Chapter 11)**
The future of LLMOps and building an LLMOps culture within organizations.

The appendix includes an MLflow CLI reference, a troubleshooting guide, and a glossary.


# Co-authored with MLflow Developers

Among the co-authors are engineers who serve as MLflow's tech lead driving observability and evaluation feature development, a developer contributing to MLflow and the DSPy AI agent framework, and a contributor since 2019 who now serves as a project maintainer.

The result is a book informed not just by user experience but by the people who design and build MLflow itself — including the reasoning behind design decisions that often go unexplained in documentation.


# Who This Book Is For

- Engineers who want to take LLM applications beyond the prototype stage
- Teams that have something working but are struggling with quality, cost, or operational stability
- MLflow users who haven't yet adopted its GenAI-focused features
- Tech leads and architects looking to establish LLMOps practices and culture within their teams
